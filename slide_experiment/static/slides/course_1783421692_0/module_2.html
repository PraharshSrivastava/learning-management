AI Fundamentals: The Role of Data

  • AI requires data to function, much like a car needs fuel.
  • Data includes facts, numbers, and items AI uses for learning.
  • Structured data is organized (e.g., Excel spreadsheets).
  • Unstructured data includes photos, emails, and videos. [IMAGE: img_24]
  • AI performance is directly tied to the quality of its input data.

Programming Paradigms: Rules vs. Learning

Option A

  • Traditional: Relies on humans defining strict, predefined rules for execution.

Option B

  • Modern (ML): Computers autonomously determine rules by analyzing vast amounts of data.

The Three Pillars of AI Training

Supervised Learning

Uses labeled data to make predictions, such as forecasting house prices from examples.

Unsupervised Learning

Finds hidden patterns in unlabeled data, like grouping users by music taste.

Reinforcement Learning

Trains AI via trial and error, guided by rewards and penalties.

Deep Learning & Neural Networks

  • Deep Learning: Advanced specialization within Machine Learning.
  • Artificial Neural Networks mimic the human brain's structure, functioning like sequential digital filters. [IMAGE: img_23]

Deep Learning: Hierarchical Feature Extraction

Basic Feature Detection

Initial layers identify fundamental elements like lines and edges in input data.

Shape Recognition

Mid-layers combine these simple features to form complex structures, such as eyes or noses.

Pattern Identification

The final layer synthesizes these shapes to recognize complete, high-level concepts, like a full human face.
